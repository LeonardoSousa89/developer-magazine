## 1. Invitation

I am building **Developer Magazine**, an international platform for
people who create technology. I am looking for two committed
collaborators to build the product with me as an independent,
cross-border team.

This is not intended to be another generic social network or a
conventional developer portfolio directory. The platform will connect
**people, projects, and knowledge** in one collaborative environment. A
professional should be discoverable through the work they have actually
created or contributed to---not only through a job title or a list of
skills.

I will lead the **Frontend Engineering** work. I am inviting:

1.  **Backend Engineer --- Node.js / TypeScript**
2.  **Database, Infrastructure & DevOps Engineer --- PostgreSQL /
    MongoDB / Cloud & CI/CD**

The goal is to share authorship, technical decisions, project credit,
and---if the product becomes commercially successful---future proceeds
under a written agreement accepted by all founding contributors.

This is an early-stage, collaborative venture. It is not a salaried job
offer, and no immediate payment or revenue is promised. We will agree on
scope, time commitment, ownership, and commercial terms before treating
the collaboration as a formal partnership.

## 2. The Product

### 2.1 Vision

**Developer Magazine is a collaborative network and living knowledge
base for the technology community.**

It will bring together:

-   Software developers and engineers
-   Computer scientists and researchers
-   Systems analysts and architects
-   Students and emerging professionals
-   Open-source maintainers and technical creators
-   Historical figures and influential professionals in computing

The platform should help people discover meaningful work, understand how
technology was built, connect with contributors, document technical
history, and create opportunities for collaboration, employment, and new
projects.

### 2.2 The three pillars

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Pillar                  Purpose                 Examples
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  **People**              Professional and        Ada Lovelace, Alan
                          historical profiles     Turing, Linus Torvalds,
                                                  current developers and
                                                  researchers

  **Projects**            Products, inventions,   Linux, Facebook, an

                          open-source work        developer's production
                                                  application

  **Knowledge**           Collaborative technical Concepts, technical
                          documentation and       articles, project
                          history                 timelines, references,
                                                  and revision history
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.3 Projects are first-class entities

Projects must have the same strategic importance as people. A project is
not merely a card attached to a profile.

A project page should be able to document:

-   What the project does and why it exists
-   Its creators, maintainers, teams, and other contributors
-   The roles and contributions of each person
-   Technologies, architecture, and relevant technical decisions
-   Releases, milestones, and its evolution over time
-   Related articles, references, and external resources
-   Evidence supporting claims about ownership or contribution

For example, a professional profile may connect to an e-commerce
platform, an open-source library, and a research project. A historical
project may connect to its original creators and later contributors. The
platform should make these relationships understandable and traceable.

### 2.4 Profile and contribution model

The initial product model distinguishes between two broad profile types:

**Public and historical profiles** - Profiles of notable living
professionals and historical figures. - Authenticated community members
may propose edits and additions. - Changes must be reviewable,
attributable, and reversible. - Sources and evidence should be
encouraged or required according to the claim type. - The platform must
distinguish verified facts, sourced claims, and disputed information.

**Personal professional profiles** - Owned and edited by the account
holder. - The initial eligibility policy is that a person must
demonstrate at least one real contribution, such as a production
project, a released product, or a verifiable open-source contribution. -
The team must define a fair review process, acceptable evidence, privacy
protections, and an appeal path before launch. - A production project
should not be the only possible route if an equivalent, meaningful
contribution can be verified.

These policies are product requirements to design and validate---not
assumptions that should be implemented without privacy, abuse, and
fairness review.

## 4. Technical Architecture

The initial architecture should be modular, documented, testable, and
appropriate for a small team. We should avoid premature microservices. A
modular monolith is the recommended starting point for the backend, with
clear domain boundaries and the option to extract services later when
real scale or organizational needs justify it.

### 4.1 High-level architecture

``` text
                         DEVELOPER MAGAZINE
                                  |
                 +----------------+----------------+
                 |                                 |
          WEB APPLICATION                    MOBILE APPLICATION
          Next.js + TypeScript               React Native + TS
                 |                                 |
                 +----------------+----------------+
                                  |
                           HTTPS / REST API
                                  |
                       BACKEND APPLICATION
                     Node.js + Express + TS
                                  |
              +-------------------+-------------------+
              |                   |                   |
          PostgreSQL           MongoDB           External Services
        Core relational      Metrics/events      Email, storage,
        application data     where justified     integrations
              |
       Backups / Migrations

       CI/CD -> Test -> Build -> Deploy -> Monitor
```

### 4.2 Technology responsibilities

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Layer                   Initial technology      Responsibility
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  Web                     Next.js + TypeScript    Public pages and
                                                  authenticated web
                                                  experience

  Mobile                  React Native +          Native mobile
                          TypeScript              experience

  API                     Node.js + Express +     Business rules and
                          TypeScript              application interfaces

  Primary database        PostgreSQL              Users, profiles,
                                                  projects,
                                                  relationships,
                                                  permissions, revisions

  Metrics / event data    MongoDB                 Selected event and
                                                  metrics workloads,
                                                  subject to validation

  UI styling              Tailwind CSS + design   Shared visual language
                          tokens                  

  Containers              Docker                  Reproducible
                                                  development and
                                                  deployment environments

  CI/CD                   To be selected by the   Automated checks and
                          team                    releases

  Observability           To be selected by the   Logs, metrics, traces,
                          team                    and alerts
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Database principle:** PostgreSQL is the source of truth for core
relational entities and transactional workflows. MongoDB is not
automatically required for every metric or event. We will validate the
actual data volume, query patterns, retention needs, and operational
cost before deciding what belongs in MongoDB. Avoid duplicating
authoritative business data across databases.

### 4.3 Initial backend modules

1.  Identity & Access
2.  User and Professional Profiles
3.  Projects and Project Contributions
4.  Knowledge Articles and References
5.  Collaborative Editing and Revision History
6.  Moderation and Reporting
7.  Search and Discovery
8.  Notifications
9.  Metrics and Audit Events

Each module should have explicit responsibilities, validation rules,
authorization checks, and tests.

### 4.4 Engineering standards

-   TypeScript strict mode wherever practical.
-   Clear separation between domain logic, application services,
    infrastructure, and transport.
-   Input validation at API boundaries.
-   Explicit authorization checks on every protected operation.
-   Database migrations committed to version control.
-   No secrets committed to source control.
-   Consistent formatting, linting, naming, and commit conventions.
-   Pull requests with descriptions, review, and automated checks.
-   Unit tests for business rules; integration tests for database/API
    behavior; end-to-end tests for critical user journeys.
-   Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) for decisions with long-term
    impact.
-   English for source code identifiers, technical documentation,
    issues, and commit messages.

## 6. Product Scope for the First Beta

The beta should validate the core idea rather than attempt to ship every
future feature.

### Must-have

-   Account registration and sign-in.
-   A basic eligibility and contribution-evidence workflow for personal
    professional profiles.
-   Public professional and historical profiles.
-   Project pages with contributor relationships.
-   Create and edit proposals for collaborative public content.
-   Revision history and attribution.
-   Basic references and evidence links.
-   Search for people and projects.
-   Responsive web experience.
-   Light and Dark themes.
-   Basic moderation and reporting.
-   Logging, monitoring, backups, and security fundamentals.

### Later iterations

-   Full-featured social feed and richer interaction model.
-   Advanced project collaboration tools.
-   Sophisticated recommendation systems.
-   Advanced analytics and dashboards.
-   Expanded multilingual coverage.
-   Native mobile release after the key web workflows have been
    validated.
-   Monetization features, if validated by users and agreed by the
    founding team.

The initial beta may be web-first while the architecture remains
mobile-compatible. The team should explicitly decide whether a mobile
beta is necessary before committing to a delivery date.

## 7. Collaboration Model

### 7.1 Working principles

-   International and remote-friendly collaboration.
-   English as the default language for project communication and
    documentation.
-   Written decisions rather than undocumented verbal agreements.
-   Respectful technical disagreement and evidence-based decisions.
-   Small, reviewable pull requests.
-   Shared ownership of product quality.
-   Transparent workload and progress.
-   No contributor should be expected to work unlimited unpaid hours.

### 7.2 Suggested workflow

  Activity        Suggested practice
  --------------- ---------------------------------------------
  Planning        Shared issue tracker and milestone board
  Communication   One async-first team channel
  Documentation   Version-controlled Markdown
  Code review     At least one review before merging
  Releases        Versioned releases and documented changes
  Meetings        Short, scheduled meetings only when useful
  Decisions       ADRs for architecture and product decisions
  QA              Acceptance criteria before implementation

### 7.3 Proposed contribution rhythm

The initial collaboration should be sustainable and agreed by all
members. Each person should state their realistic weekly availability
before milestones are committed.

A possible cadence: - One planning session per milestone. - Weekly
asynchronous progress updates. - Pull requests reviewed as contributors
have capacity. - A short demo or review at the end of each milestone. -
Retrospectives to improve the workflow.

No fixed weekly-hour commitment is assumed by this proposal.

## 8. Recognition, Ownership & Future Revenue

This project is intended to be built collaboratively. The founding
contributors should receive visible and fair recognition for the work
they deliver.

### 8.1 Credits

The proposed founding team consists of:

-   **Frontend Engineer / Web & Mobile Lead**
-   **Backend Engineer**
-   **Database, Infrastructure & DevOps Engineer**

All three should be credited as founding technical contributors, with
their actual responsibilities and contributions clearly documented.

Credits should appear in appropriate project documentation and, when
agreed, on the product's About / Team page. Public credit must
accurately reflect work performed.

### 8.2 Proposed economic principle

If Developer Magazine becomes profitable, the founding contributors
intend to share the economic benefits under a written agreement.

**Discussion proposal:** an equal one-third allocation of the founders'
agreed distributable profit pool:

  Founding role                                  Proposed share
  -------------------------------------------- ----------------
  Frontend Engineer / Web & Mobile Lead                  33.33%
  Backend Engineer                                       33.33%
  Database, Infrastructure & DevOps Engineer             33.33%

This is a starting proposal for negotiation---not a legally binding
promise or an automatic entitlement. The final agreement must define
what "profit" means and how the pool is calculated.

Before revenue distribution, the agreement should specify: - Whether
shares refer to company equity, net profit, revenue, or another economic
right. - Which operating expenses, taxes, payment fees, refunds, and
reserves are deducted. - How additional contributors and future hires
are compensated. - Whether shares vest over time or against agreed
milestones. - What happens if a founding contributor leaves, becomes
inactive, or materially breaches the agreement. - How ownership, voting,
and major decisions work. - How intellectual property and pre-existing
work are treated. - How disputes, deadlocks, and termination are
handled. - Which legal entity and jurisdiction will govern the
arrangement.

**Important:** Equal credit does not by itself establish equal legal
ownership. Because the team may be international, all members should
review and sign a founders' / collaboration agreement before commercial
launch or transfer of valuable intellectual property. Obtain qualified
legal and tax advice for the relevant countries.

### 8.3 Intellectual property and open-source policy

Before the first substantial contribution, the team should agree in
writing on: - Ownership or licensing of code, designs, documentation,
and brand assets. - Treatment of each member's pre-existing libraries,
tools, and portfolio work. - Third-party dependencies and license
compliance. - Whether and when any project components may be
open-sourced. - Contributor attribution and permission to display work
in portfolios. - Confidential information and security-sensitive
material.

No contributor should assume that all work is automatically assigned to
a company or that a permissive open-source license applies. These terms
must be explicit.

## 9. Security, Privacy & Trust

The platform's credibility depends on the integrity of its profiles and
project histories.

The team should design for: - Data minimization and privacy by
default. - Secure password and session handling. - Role-based
authorization and least privilege. - Protection against spam,
impersonation, abuse, and malicious edits. - Audit trails for meaningful
content changes. - Source attribution and correction workflows. - Backup
and recovery testing. - Secure secret storage and dependency updates. -
Privacy-aware analytics and retention policies. - Applicable data
protection requirements in the markets where the product operates.

The platform should never imply that a contribution, employment history,
credential, or project ownership has been independently verified unless
the verification process supports that claim.
